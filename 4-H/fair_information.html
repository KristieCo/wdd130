Goals: 4-H goals with 3 things to do to obtain those goals.

  • Junior (8-10)- 1 goal
  • Intermediate (11-13)- 2 goals
  • Senior (14-18)- 3 goals
Presentation: Your child will need to add their presentation to their record book. So, scan or add the file that they used or take a picture of what they did as in a picture of their poster. There is a place to do a little write up as well. I have tried to get pictures of all of the kids while presenting and will send a link to the google album. If I did not get a picture to you and they have already done it, you could always take some photos looking like they are presenting to someone or just add scans of what they did.

Expense records: Keep track of all of the expenses that you have had with these animals. These go in their record books so they are aware of cost and profit in the end.

Medical records: Keep track of any medical things with the animals as well. If you give them meds, write down days and what it was. Also needed for record books. The big one here is Kaopectate if they have diarrhea or deworming medicine. Call any time if you have questions or concerns. If at all possible do not deworm your animals closer than two weeks before the fair starts. If your animal starts coughing, (not just once or twice, a perpetual cough all day) we need to get them looked at by a vet. Make sure that their pen and food are never dusty. This can cause major problems that can lead to them not being able to go to the fair. I use Upper Valley Vet out on the way to Wal-Mart.

Weight: Keep track of the different weights that you get for your animal. This will be added to the record book as well. I get a picture or write the weights down in June and keep track of weights when we do club weights so that kids can add them to their books.

Pictures: Take pictures throughout the summer and you can add them to their books as well.

4-H Story: Your kids will need to write a story about their year in 4-H. About getting their animals and all that they did this year. Here are the required lengths. So yes minimum 1 page, but I would have them make it a little longer if they are older and have done 4-H for a few years. But Shannon and I will have to read them, so no need to do the maximum amount of pages. Junior(8-10)- 1-4 pages Intermediate(11-13)- 1-6 pages Senior(14-18)- 1-8 pages

Bill of Sale/Proof of Ownership and Born and Raised: Needs to be in the child's name, not the parents. So, if you get one with the parent's name on it, you can than fill out another one transferring ownership to the child or just make sure it is the right name to begin with. The Bill of Sale or Proof of Ownership along with the Born and Raised in Madison County sheets need to be turned into Shay at the 4-H office or emailed to her no later than May 30th which is one week before our scheduled weigh in. If you do not get this submitted to her, your child will not be able to show their animal at the fair. If you have alternate (extra animals for just in case) they need to also have their paperwork submitted and need to be to the beginning weigh in in June.

Prepare for Show: At the fair, the children need to be the one to get their animal or goal ready to show in the ring. Yes, they can have adult assistance, but they want the kids learning and doing that. So, practicing now would be a great thing. Washing lambs and practicing fluffing their leg hair right now could be a good thing. Not just walking their animals, which is very important to be doing now as well. When working with their animals this summer, it will be best to walk them during the cooler part of the day such as early morning or evening. I also like how they described at one of our clinics when walking them. We want a body builder build and not a marathoner build. So, we want short burst of work outs and not long prolonged workouts. We want them to build muscle and still have a layer of fat, but not too big of a layer of fat.

Buyers: Be thinking of potential businesses that you work with or people who could buy lambs. We need more buyers in our pool for the sale. You will need to reach out to them before the fair and get information to them as well as other people that you would think would add money onto your child animal.

Record Books: Record books are due to us mid-July. Talk with us if you have questions or need an extension. We need time to go through them to make sure that they are finished before the 4-H office gets them and grades them. This is a must for kids to participate in the fair. With the book you will need to submit the record book and involvement report along with the county materials. These county materials are not in the record book but can be found in their folder or on zsuite. These will be scanned and then added to the record book. Also get pictures put in.

Buyer Photos and Fliers: Buyer photos are due to Shay mid-July. You should have get an email from Shay with details. Let me know if you did not get it and I will forward it to you. Shay will also send the sale flyer with information about the livestock sale and boosters which is great to share with your child's flyer. These are good to make and print and take to people and businesses that can be potential buyers or will add money on your child's animal after the sale. These are great to share with businesses that you work with such as banks, insurance, feed places, grandparents, rich Uncle's, the sky is the limit. The worst thing that can happen is for them not to do anything about helping your child. There should also be booster or add on information with that flyer too which would be good to share with these people as well. Make a goal of sending out or visiting at least 10 businesses or people with these.

Fair Posters: Each child needs to make a poster by hand or printed with their picture with their animal and names at the very least. Some people put weights and other things on it as well. Simple or fancy, it is up to you. These will hang above pens at the fairgrounds to people can know who is who.

Show Registration: This will need done online before the fair. Shay will send information on it a few weeks before the fair and we will make sure that everyone is taken care of.

Club meetings info for record book: Dates and what happened and number of people there including 4h member or not Presentation topics. I will put a picture of the list online on the google photo album that I shared. If I have something wrong on it please let me know and I will send an updated one.

Fair Expectations: Tuesday 8/11-Saturday 8/15- Plan to come to the fairgrounds a minimum of twice each day to check on and take care of animals. Pens will need to be cleaned daily (before 8 is best) or more often if needed. Animals will need fed and watered twice each day as well. Thursday 8/13- Lamb show day. Saturday 8/15- Livestock Sale Day. It starts around 8:30. Then clean up pens and put animals on trailer about 5:00 that evening.

Fair Set Up: Fair pen set up. Saturday, August 8th at 8:00 am. All who are in town need to come help. It should take less than an hour. Many hands make light work. Bring scissors and or wire cutter. We use zip ties to put them together and the ends will need cut off. We should get pens assigned that day and you will know where your animals will be.

Livestock Judging: Livestock judging is Friday afternoon of fair week. I think the kids need to register online for this if they would like to particiapte. It is a great opportunity to learn and possibly go to state as well. But with this, they select at least 4 animals of each species to use in the ring. They randomly select animals and make sure they have varying quality so that the kids can understand the differences. I guess that people have been upset when their animals have been taken without being asked and feel like their animal was being made fun of or miss treated. They said that they will do better and ask before taking and respect what you say, but they do not do it to cause harm or be rude. So just be aware that this will be happening. Chances are low in it being any of our animals used because there are so many lambs, but be aware and don’t get upset.

Final Weigh-in Day: Tuesday, August 11th final weigh in. I will find out our time soon but it will most likely be between 5 and 6. Your animals can be brought in any time after 8:00 that moreing and we can start decorating our area. The theme is Honoring the Past, Growing the Future. If our decorating committee needs help, let me know. This is a great day to put posters up as well. It can be as simple as a picture with the child and animals name on it or as elaborate as you would like. I usually do about ½ poster size. Just a reminder that after animals are weighed, they will have an ultrasound taken. They want us to take them on the grass and through the East doors into the building and then over by the arena they will be doing the ultrasounds. It should only take a few seconds for each animal. When kids get their checks there will be an $8 charge taken out for this along with the I think $10 charge that they already remove. Prizes will be awarded to the top 3 animals.

Extra and underweight animals: Anyone who has an extra animal that they want to sell to the floor buyer, that animal will go on a trailer after it is weighed at the final weigh-in and not left at the fairgrounds. A check will be given to you for them in a few weeks. What if my animal does not make weight? You have a few options. Sell it to the floor buyer right then and leave the fair grounds and not show at all. It will be gone Tuesday night and you will get a check for it in a few weeks. And be prepared, it may be less than what you bought the lamb for. With this option your child will not get any money for their animal besides what you get from the floor buyer. Sell it to the floor buyer later that week. Plan to have your child show their animal in showmanship and not quality because it has not made weight and cannot be auctioned. But they can still go to the floor buyer and will be put on a trailer Thursday night or Saturday with the others. If the kids show their animal and get add-ons put on their account, they can get a check. So, if they take flyers around and people know they are showing and donate to them, the kids will get that money. You can even let potential buyers/donators know that the animal can not be sold and they may possibly be generous like many are and their check can be pretty good. If you sell to the floor buyer, they pay hoof weight which is the national rate and that is why it could be less than you bought it for this spring, but it is something. Sell your animal privately. If you do not want to sell to the floor buyer you can take your lamb home on Tuesday and keep it or sell it yourself. But if kids keep them at the fair grounds and show them in showmanship, they have a chance of getting add-ons if people know about their animal. But if you want to keep it or sell it privately, you will need to get it home Thursday evening if that makes sense.

Saturday, August 16th: I need at least one item from our club for the pre-auction. We could do something together or just one family does it. It can be a baked good, homemade item, or a gift basket. If there is someone who wants to do this let me know. Saturday, August 15th market sale starts at 9:00 sharp. We get there before 8 to get pens and animals cleaned up. There will be an auction list and then you can have an idea of when your child will sale their animal. It takes forever, but goes quick when they are in the ring. Once your children’s animals are sold, you can leave until pen clean up once animals are taken care of if you would like. Plan for this to last until early afternoon. Saturday, August 15th pen clean up. At 4:00 we can start taking down decorations and posters and getting ready for animals to get loaded into the trailer. The trailer will be there at 5:00 to take them. We need everyone there to help until it is done. The barn needs to be clean before anyone can leave.

Buyer Thank you's and Checks: Under no circumstances can anyone bring a thank you to buyers during the auction. You can talk to them after the whole auction is done. But better to wait until that next week. Listen to who buys your child’s animal and prepare to take them a thank you card and the poster from Shay a week or two afterwards. You can call Shay a week or so after the fair to get info from her if needed as well. Just not during the auction. Other thank you notes and check info. I guess buyers and people doing add-ons are not getting thank you notes from the kids. This is a requirement for getting their checks. I want to take your word for it that you send them, but could be like other leaders and have you show me proof before I will get you your child’s check. A few weeks after the fair I will get paperwork saying who is paying what to your child and get it to you. They will then need to do their notes and send them. But once they get their check, there could be potential additional add-ons so check that paperwork against the first one and send additional notes if needed. I will be given checks and check with you and get them to you. So, no need to ask Shay about this. They are hoping the first checks will be out before the end of September. And some take until the end of November as well. Just depends on when they get the money from the people. But I will keep you updated.

Buying animals at the fair: What if I want to buy my child’s animal but do not want to pay the auction price for it. Once your child’s animal is sold through the auction, the buyer can choose to keep it and you have no rights to it again. No asking them to buy it, it is theirs and they can do whatever they want with it. But with lambs, the majority of them get sold back to the floor buyer. And if this happens, parents have the first option of buying that animal back. How do I do this? You will need to call or email Shay and she will register you as a buyer or you can register on the computer as a buyer at the fairgrounds. This needs done by Friday night. They claim that you can only buy one of your childs animals back, but they did let me buy two last year so just ask. So, if you are considering making your own flock or filling your freezer, make some decisions before the fair and get the information to Shay. If you are considering buying a pig, goat, or beef to go in your freezer, you will also need to register as a buyer and sign up to get one. They will just draw numbers out of a hat and you can choose to keep that animal or not. If you have further questions with this, let me know.

Children at the Fair: Keep track of all of your children while at the fair. I guess there has been a problem with kids running around wild at the fair and causing problems with vendors or children getting lost. Make sure you know where they are and they are being respectful of others. I guess things have been taken from vendors and it is starting to become a problem.

Animal Care at the Fair: Pens need cleaned daily. It is good to do this early so that we are not disturbing shows going on in the ring. Part of clean up, if your animal poops on the grass or outside of their pens at the fairgrounds, be prepared to clean it up. Animals need fed like they have been all summer. Hopefully morning and night. They may need extra water because the barn gets pretty warm with everyone in there. Getting ready for the show and sale. Kids are supposed to be the ones getting animals ready to show with minimal parental help. If you need a supply list, we can discuss this. Yes, parents can help, just not do all of the work. I would encourage you to have animals ready to go the Monday before the fair so that you have minimal last-minute things to do at the fairgrounds. Such as fluffing legs, final trimming, and putting black on their legs if you choose to do that.

Fair Uniform: Show uniform. White button shirt, long pants preferably jeans with no holes, sturdy shoes that are not falling apart, hair done nicely and up for girls. Sale uniform. If kids would like to wear something besides their white shirt they can, just be dressed nicely. The majority of kids just wear their white shirt. But you can dress yourself or animal up if wanted. And you can wear a hat.

Show Schedule They will post the order of who shows a little bit before the shows start on the table and or wall by the front entrance. I will also help make sure you are aware of when your kids are about ready to show. Lots is going on that day. Thursday 8-12 lamb showmanship show. Thursday 1-6 lamb quality show. There is a chance that some of the kids could be in the born and raised contest. We shall see how it goes and keep you informed if your children will be showing in this after quality.

Round Robin and Awards: Friday 4:30- 5:15 Round Robin Contest. Top showman for each species does this but it is fun to watch and kids can learn a lot if they are not one of the top showman. Friday 5:30-6:30 Awards Ceremony. These kids that win belt buckles or banner will be recognized and awarded. It is great to support the kids if you are able to be there.
